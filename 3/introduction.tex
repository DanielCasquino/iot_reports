\IEEEPARstart{T}{his} laboratory approaches the connection between data acquisition on an ESP32 and message distribution using MQTT. The experiments cover connectivity, telemetry, control and serial integration.

\subsection{Theoretical Framework}
MQTT uses the publisher--subscriber model: a client publishes to a topic, and the server distributes messages according to subscriptions.
MQTT 3.1.1 requires an ordered, bidirectional transport, like TCP. Its QoS level~0 offers delivery at most once; it does not guarantee that every message will reach the receiver \cite{mqtt311}. Therefore, simply observing a connection is not enough to quantify message loss or receipt of sensor readings.

In the Arduino-ESP32 API, \texttt{analogRead} returns a raw, uncalibrated ADC code, while \texttt{analogReadMilliVolts} provides a calibrated result in millivolts \cite{esp32adc}. An ADC code obtained from a divider with an LDR does not directly equate to illuminance in lux. The relation
with illumination requires characterizing the sensor, circuit, and reference conditions; this conversion is not performed here.

PubSubClient 2.8 selects MQTT 3.1.1 by default. Its overload \texttt{publish(topic,payload)} constructs a message without retention and without QoS bits~1/2; \texttt{loop()} handles client communication \cite{pubsub28}. These properties describe the library and allow interpretation of the CP2 sketch; they do not replace a receiver capture.

\subsection{State of the Art}
MQTT is commonly studied as a lightweight application-layer protocol for
resource-constrained IoT devices. Al-Fuqaha \emph{et al.} identify
publish--subscribe protocols, including MQTT, as an important part of the
IoT protocol stack and relate protocol selection to device constraints,
network conditions, and application requirements \cite{alfuqaha2015}.
This perspective is relevant to the present experiment because the ESP32
acts as a constrained sensing client while the broker separates the
publisher from the monitoring application.

Several studies compare MQTT with alternatives rather than evaluating it
in isolation. Thangavel \emph{et al.} evaluated MQTT and CoAP through a
common middleware and showed that their behavior depends on the
communication pattern and transport assumptions \cite{thangavel2014}.
Similarly, the experimental study by Borsatti \emph{et al.}
examined MQTT performance in IoT-to-cloud communication and reported that
the selected QoS level affects latency and application behavior
\cite{borsatti2020}. These results support documenting the protocol
parameters instead of treating a successful connection as a complete
performance evaluation.

For low-power wireless sensor networks, Hunkeler, Truong, and
Stanford-Clark proposed MQTT-S as an adaptation of the MQTT model to
networks where conventional TCP connectivity is not always available
\cite{mqtts}. More recent work by Palmese, Redondi, and Cesana proposed
adaptive QoS control for MQTT-SN and evaluated its effect on delay and
packet errors \cite{palmese2022}. In contrast, this laboratory uses
standard MQTT over Wi-Fi/TCP and QoS~0 through PubSubClient; therefore,
the experiment demonstrates basic telemetry but does not claim the
reliability or energy behavior of MQTT-SN.

Recent measurements also investigate alternatives to TCP. Jeddou \emph{et al.}
characterized delay and energy consumption for MQTT over TCP and
QUIC using commercial off-the-shelf devices \cite{jeddou2022}. Their
results reinforce that latency and energy depend on the complete
transport stack and hardware, not only on the MQTT message format. The
present work is consequently positioned as an implementation and
functional validation of an ESP32--MQTT telemetry path. It provides
evidence of acquisition and delivery, but it does not estimate lux,
compare protocols statistically, or measure energy consumption.
