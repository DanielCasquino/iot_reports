This section analyzes eight scientific works relevant to the project context. Topics include home energy management and technology adoption by users, measurement and disaggregation of electric loads, and low cost implementations with an ESP32 microcontroller.

\subsection*{A1. A Review of Smart Home Applications Based on Internet of Things}
Alla et al.~\cite{alaa2017} conduct a systematic review about the IoT-based smart home ecosystem, constructing a taxonomy of applications and stating that current research is scattered and presents several challenges in interoperability, costs, and user experience. This work establishes the importance of low cost, integrated solutions, and provides an application classification framework (monitoring + control + management).

\subsection*{A2. A Review of Internet of Things for Smart Home: Challenges and Solutions}
Stojkoska \& Trivodaliev~\cite{stojkoska2017} review the technologies that enable smart homes, as well as their challenges ---conectivity, security, enegy consumption, and device heterogenuity---, establising architecture and protocol choice as critical decisions. This work inspired the use of low-power, low-cost technologies, such as the ESP32 microcontroller and the MQTT protocol.

\subsection*{A3. Energy Conservation through Smart Homes in a Smart City: A Lesson for Singapore Households}
Bhati, Hansen \& Chan~\cite{bhati2017} study the impact that smart home technologies have on energy conservation in Singaporean homes, showing that feedback and automation are important contributing factors, and that this impact is strenghtened with the interconnection of devices, homes, and electrical networks. This paper backs up the project proposal by establising precedents on energy management and the importance of user feedback.

\subsection*{A4. Categories and Functionality of Smart Home Technology for Energy Management}
Ford, Pritoni, Sanguinetti \& Karlin~\cite{ford2017} analyze 308 commercial products that target home energy management, classify their functionality, and show the existing gap between product description and their actual capabilities. This work justifies prioritizing feature quality over quantity.

\subsection*{A5. A Systematic Review of the Smart Home Literature: A User Perspective}
Marikyan, Papagiannidis \& Alamanos~\cite{marikyan2019} review literature from a user perspective, identifying perceived usefulness, trust, privacy, and ease-of-use as deciding factors for user adoption. This work guides decision regarding UI (user interface) and enables the possibility of integrating a virtual assistant to provide simple and comprehensible interaction.

\subsection*{A6. Non-Intrusive Load Monitoring Approaches for Disaggregated Energy Sensing: A Survey}
Zoha, Gluhak, Imran, and Rajasegarar~\cite{zoha2012} present a review of non-intrusive load monitoring (NILM) techniques, describing how current and power enable the identification of the status and operation of equipment. The project applies this idea to a single, well-characterized load (the water heater), using its electrical signature to infer heating and detect anomalies.

\subsection*{A7. Electric Energy Management in the Smart Home: Perspectives on Enabling Technologies and Consumer Behavior}
Zipperer and collaborators~\cite{zipperer2013} analyze enabling technologies and consumer behavior in home energy management, concluding that success depends as much on the technical component as on the user's willingness to interact with the information. This reinforces the need for solutions that combine measurement, control, and a user-friendly interface.

\subsection*{A8. A Smart Energy Monitoring System Using ESP32 Microcontroller}
El-Khozondar and collaborators~\cite{elkhozondar2024} develop a low-cost energy monitoring system based on the ESP32 microcontroller, integrating voltage sensors (ZMPT101B) and current sensors (SCT-013), and reporting data to the user via the network, demonstrating the viability of the ESP32 and current transformers for home electrical monitoring. This work is the closest precedent to the electrical measurement component of the project.

\subsection{Study Comparison and Identified Gap}

Table ~\ref{tab:arte} summarizes the analyzed works. Sources (A1--A5) cover energy management and user adoption, while works (A6--A8) study load measurement and low cost implementations.

However, there is space for solutions specifically tailored towards \textbf{domestic water heaters} that simultaneously integrate temperature, curent, and flow measurement, safe power actuators (SSR), wireless communication with preprocessing, and an interface that combines comprehensible dashboards and virtual assistants.
