\subsection{Reference Appliance and Components}
The reference is the Rheem EW1-50RP, a 50 L, 1500 W, 220--240 V storage heater. Its panel already shows tank-center temperature and controls heating~\cite{rheemmanual}. The proposed add-on uses exactly three sensors and one actuator, listed in Table~\ref{tab:components}. The ESP32-WROOM controller and ESP32-CAM camera node are development boards, not additional sensors or actuators.

\subsection{Architecture and Operating Sequence}
Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} separates sensing and low-voltage actuation from the heater's existing mains supply. The camera node transfers a cropped image of the visor to the application service for digit recognition. The controller counts Hall pulses, derives flow and accumulated volume, and classifies color readings under a light shield. MQTT over Wi-Fi carries telemetry and on/off requests between the ESP32 controller and application service; the camera sends images over Wi-Fi for recognition. The application shows the latest valid temperature, pilot state, and water use, marking stale or unreadable data instead of treating it as current.

For a requested state change, the controller first checks a recent pilot observation. It issues one short servo press only when the observed state differs from the requested state, then retracts and waits for a new observation. A dark or ambiguous pilot does not by itself prove that the heater is off. If the state cannot be confirmed, the application reports uncertainty and asks for manual verification; it does not keep toggling. Reading the numeric visor may require waking the panel, but the button's wake-versus-toggle behavior is not documented and will be tested before any automated wake press is enabled. A local manual press remains possible after removing the pusher, and loss of Wi-Fi does not alter the heater's original operation.

\subsection{Calibration and Validation}
The proposed enclosure uses a detachable support to keep the camera, color sensor, and servo aligned with the panel. Commercial button pushers such as SwitchBot Bot inform the external press-and-release mechanism~\cite{switchbot}; their compatibility is not assumed for this heater. The support must allow removal for manual access. Its attachment, dimensions, and servo travel will be selected from the panel geometry and button tests. The detachable panel module is distinct from the flow meter, whose installation requires opening and resealing an external plumbing union. The proposed YF-S201 has nominal male G1/2 connections~\cite{seeedflow}; the size and thread type of the heater's flexible connection have not been measured. Before procurement, mating fittings, available space, pressure and temperature ratings, and a location clear of the relief-valve discharge must be verified. An adapter or a different meter may be required.

The camera is aligned and shielded against glare. Recognized digits are compared with the visible panel over several temperatures; blank images and heater error codes are marked unavailable. The color classifier is checked under red, green, dark, and ambient-light conditions. The flow meter is calibrated with a measured water volume; pulses per liter and the installation point are recorded. Finally, the servo stroke and return are tested on the momentary button, and on/off requests are compared with the observed panel response. The electronics use a separate low-voltage supply inside a removable case. The case design leaves the heater's ventilation, safety discharge, and ordinary access unobstructed. The external water union must be checked for fitting size, rated pressure and temperature, leaks, and its position relative to the relief valve before installation.
