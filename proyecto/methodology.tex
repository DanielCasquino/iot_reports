\subsection{Reference Appliance and Components}
The reference is the Rheem EW1-50RP, a 50 L, 1500 W, 220--240 V storage heater. Its panel already shows tank-center temperature and controls heating~\cite{rheemmanual}. The proposed add-on uses exactly three sensors and one actuator, listed in Table~\ref{tab:components}. The ESP32-WROOM controller and ESP32-CAM camera node are development boards, not additional sensors or actuators.

\subsection{Architecture and Operating Sequence}
Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} separates sensing and low-voltage actuation from the heater's existing mains supply. The camera node transfers a cropped image of the visor to the application service for digit recognition. The controller counts Hall pulses, derives flow and volume, and classifies RGB readings under a light shield. The application shows the latest valid temperature, pilot state, and water use.

For a requested state change, the controller first checks a recent panel observation. It issues one short servo press only when the observed state differs from the requested state, then retracts and waits for a new observation. If the visor or pilot is unreadable, the application reports an uncertain state and asks for manual verification; it does not keep toggling. A local manual press remains possible after removing the pusher, and loss of Wi-Fi does not alter the heater's original operation.

\subsection{Calibration and Validation}
The camera is aligned and shielded against glare. Recognized digits are compared with the visible panel over several temperatures; blank images and heater error codes are marked unavailable. The RGB classifier is checked under red, green, and ambient-light conditions. The flow meter is calibrated with a measured water volume; pulses per liter and the installation point are recorded. Finally, the servo stroke and return are tested on the momentary button, and on/off requests are compared with the observed panel response. The electronics use a separate low-voltage supply. Installation of the external water union must be checked for pressure, temperature, fittings, and leaks before household use.
